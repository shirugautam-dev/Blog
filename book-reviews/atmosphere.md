After reading Evidence of the Affair by Taylor Jenkins Reid, I knew I had to read Atmosphere. Everyone was raving about it. I waited some time before I could start, as I already had three books running at the time, and I also wanted it to be a treat that I saved for last. But this one did not turn out as I expected. I desperately wanted this one to be good, and maybe I expected too much.

The story starts with the most climactic, pivotal scene, which should have had me in its grip, but it didn’t. 

I kept on reading, hoping to feel the emotions the author was trying to evoke in readers. There was none to come. At about 40% of the book, I even asked ChatGPT when it was going to be interesting. It told me that it is slow in the beginning; later it becomes intense. I finished the book never feeling that way even once. 

The story is set in the 1980s, during NASA’s Space Shuttle program, and progresses to a catastrophic event in December 1984. Out of thousands of applicants, Joan Goodwin is chosen to be an astronaut. She has a sister, Barbara, who neglects her daughter, Frances. Joan steps in whenever she can, ultimately becoming her guardian. During her training, she meets Vanessa Ford, whom she thinks is mysterious and highly capable. Joan finds love and passion she never believed she was capable of. Joan and Vanessa explore their love and struggle to hide their relationship in the sexist, male-dominated environment. 

It is a story of two women who fall in love at a time when their love was not accepted.

<em>“Bravery is being unafraid of something other people are afraid of. Courage is being afraid, but strong enough to do it anyway.”</em>

The story follows two different times: one while they are in their training period when their relationship flourishes, and another when Vanessa goes to space, and Joan is a CAPCOM – one who communicates with the astronauts during the mission. 

The relationship between Joan and Frances is portrayed well. Barbara is easily hateable, as the author intended. The character of Lydia adds a little spice to the story, nothing more. 

The beginning was too detailed, almost feeling like a science class. There was too much information and too many characters introduced at once. But despite those details and information, I didn’t feel the tension of the space mission. 

The middle part concentrated on her relationship with Vanessa and the situation with Frances. There also something was missing. The scenes felt repetitive and predictable. Dialogues felt like lessons about the sky, stars, the universe and God, and they often felt flat.

I wanted to like this book, and I almost felt sorry for myself at the end. After all, I am her fan. 

I will read her other books for sure, but this one is an easy miss.
