    I watched Ocean Vuong on David Perell’s podcast and felt I had to read him immediately.

    After reading a few pages, I got worried that I would not even understand what he was writing. It was more like a poem, with carefully chosen words, and I got confused sometimes about whether he was talking about the present or the past.
    
    I wanted to like this book because I liked him in person, but I found it was not that simple. I was left with mixed feelings when I finished. Though the story is heart wrenching, words are beautiful, the story itself unfolds in fragments.       

    <em>“They say nothing lasts forever, but they're just scared it will last longer than they can love it.” </em>

    It is a letter written by a son, Little Dog, to his mother. But we know in the beginning itself that the mother cannot read, so the son is only pouring his heart out. The letter starts from the time he was not born, when his mother was in Vietnam before moving to the US. He writes about the family history of violence and trauma; discloses the part of his life his mother has never known.   

    As much as the writer worked, the readers also have to work to piece together the story of Little Dog, Rose and Lan, his mother and grandmother. It doesn’t come easily.

    The book gained a lot of praise, and I am sure others have enjoyed. But for me, I could not engage the way I wanted to.
