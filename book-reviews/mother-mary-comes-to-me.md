    I didn’t know what to expect from this memoir, but once I started, I could not put it down. Though I had read The God of Small Things, I had no idea about the author’s background, and that made it an excellent read. 

    It is hilarious and sad at the same time. One minute you will be shocked by a mother’s wrath, and another minute you will be laughing out loud because the author makes light of the incidents.

    <em>“The world was too ridiculous for me to remain too sad for too long.” </em>

    Arundhati Roy takes us to her childhood and introduces her mother, Mrs Roy (Mary Roy), a single mother who could not be defeated by life. She takes her brother to the highest court of India and, in the course, helps change the inheritance laws for Christian women across Kerala. But she also inflicts physical and emotional abuse on her children. The children are hurt and oppressed. The author runs away from home, but her brother stays back. We, readers, are dismayed and ask ourselves if Mary Roy could be forgiven. But the author does exactly that; she loves her even.

    It is not all sad. Her absent father enters into her adult life and adds flavour to the plot. We see the author attaining great success, and we cheer for her.

    Through this book, the author shows us how we are shaped by our childhood, our relationships and how we are ready to forget and forgive our troubled past. It is also about loving those who hurt us, living with our memories and making peace with our wounds.  
    
    The language is simple, but the meanings are not. The author softly delivers the scathing sarcasm, and we feel the pain she felt. Her brutal honesty, the willingness to bare raw, is remarkable. 

    It is a read one should not miss.
