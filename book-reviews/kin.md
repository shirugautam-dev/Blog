This book was on Barack Obama's reading list; hence I needed to read it. But when I started, I was a bit disappointed and wondered why he picked this one. The beginning was not gripping; the pace was slow, and I found it repetitive as well. I waited for it to get interesting and did not give up because I have promised myself this year that whatever I start, I will finish. It did get interesting in the second half.

This is a story of friendship, racism, inequality and self-discovery. The two main characters, Anne and Vernice, young girls, friends from childhood, are motherless, loveless and poor in 1950 America. They leave home to start new lives separately, and the novel follows their lives, both very different from each other. Anne runs away with her crush to Memphis to find her mother, and Vernice goes to Atlanta for studies. 

Anne’s life is nothing but hardship from start to finish. Vernice gets through college only to get married into a rich family. They stay connected through the letters they write to each other. 

<em>“The world wanted so much from me. Love required so much betrayal. Sometimes of myself. Oftentimes, everyone with a heart ended up devastated.”</em>

I like the way chapters alternated between Anne and Vernice, making it easy to follow. I also liked that the story unfolded through the letters, which was a good way to show how the characters were feeling. 

Can I say that this book worked for me in parts? I liked the writing style, but the story itself was not fulfilling. The ending brought together the supporting characters Lulabelle and Joette beautifully, yet I did not like the ending itself. 

All in all, it was an okay read.
