    I have read a few more novels by Ann Patchett. I have greatly enjoyed her writing. She picks seemingly simple stories and tells them in such a way that you keep on thinking about them for a long time. 
    
    Having said that, this is not my favourite out of all hers that I have read. I rank it after Tom Lake and The Dutch House. It is slower, and the plot is simpler. 
    
    It is a tender tale of Daphne, a fifty-three-year-old woman, and her stepfather, Eddie, whom she meets after forty-four years. The story unfolds the accident they had when she was nine, the reason her mother left Eddie, and the connection they both carried for a lifetime. I fell in love with Eddie right away. He is gentle and reassuring, the kind of person you would want to spend time with.
    
    The author wonderfully explores how memory, guilt and the different versions of the same event shape our lives. 
    
    <em>“I don’t know why people bother to guess at things. They’re always wrong.”</em>
    
    It tells us about the impermanence of life; the decisions we make in haste and the connections we form with each other. Can a relationship survive four decades of absence? 

    If you are an Ann Patchett fan and enjoy slow-paced stories with not much conflict, then this book is for you. Ann Patchett would delight you with her word choice and the lyrical prose as ever.

    I wondered about the title throughout the book, but by the end, I was so delighted by how perfectly it fit. I will remember Eddie for a long time.
