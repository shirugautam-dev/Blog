This is my first book by Fredrik Backman. I had not heard about him before. Again, this was an accidental find on Kindle Unlimited. I was looking for a light, quick read. But how would I know what I was in for?

Those sixty pages of humour, wisdom and sharp observations of human psychology hooked me with the author for life.

The novella explores boundaries, personal space and our relationship with the word “no”. Lucas is in his thirties, lives alone and is happy not doing anything. For him, playing video games and eating pad thai with extra peanuts are the pleasures of life. He doesn’t want to engage with anyone and wants to be left on his own. But does society let him? Of course, not.

He is, without his intention, dragged into the “Frying Pan Incident Committee” in the housing society he lives in when someone leaves a frying pan on the floor next to the recycling room.

He tries his best to get out of it, only to be dragged into it more. The society ladies, whom he remembers by their clothing colours, don’t let him escape.

The story portrays how we are torn between the societal pressures and our own needs; how difficult it is to say “no” and the conflict we bear inside.

Backman tells this deeply human story with a blend of humour and moments of introspection, which I have come to know as his signature style.

<em>“Being smart is the worst thing one can be in modern society. All it ever means is more work.”</em>

<em>“He doesn’t want enemies, but he also doesn’t want to risk making the only thing that’s worse than that: friends.”</em>

I recommend this book to those who enjoy short reads, character-driven stories and humour that explores human behaviour and the everyday inner conflicts we live with.

Though it was a quick read, it left a lasting impression on me, not only about the author, but also about the topic of self-assertion.

I am so happy I found him. Now I am excited that I have so many books to read by him 😊.
