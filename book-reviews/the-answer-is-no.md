    This is my first book by Fredrik Backman. I had not heard about him before. I picked it up only because it was on Kindle Unlimited and I was looking for a quick read. But how would I know what I was in for? 
    
    Those sixty pages of humour, wisdom and sharp observations of human psychology hooked me with the author for life. 

    <em>“Being smart is the worst thing one can be in modern society. All it ever means is more work.” </em>

    The novella explores boundaries, personal space and our relationship with the word “no”. It is about a man who doesn’t want to engage with anyone and wants to be left on his own. But does society let him? Of course, not. 
    
    He is, without his intention, dragged into a task in the housing society he lives in. He tries his best to get out of it, only to be dragged into it more. 
    
    The story portrays how we are torn between the societal pressures and our own needs; how difficult it is to say “no” and the conflict we bear inside. Backman tells this deeply human story with a blend of humour and moments of introspection, which I have come to know as his signature style.
    
    I recommend this book to those who enjoy short reads, character-driven stories and humour that explores human behaviour and the everyday inner conflicts we live with.            
    
    Though it was a quick read, it left a lasting impression on me, not only about the author, but also about the topic of self-assertion.
