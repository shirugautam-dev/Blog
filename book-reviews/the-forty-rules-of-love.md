Lately, I have been watching David Parell’s podcast on YouTube and have come to know about many authors. Elif Shafak is one of them. When I heard her, I wanted to read all her books. I have read only this to date; many more to come.

This book is written in the present and past. The past follows the life of Rumi in the 13th century, when he meets his mentor Shams of Tubriz, which leads him to become a poet and a Sufi mystic. 

Forty rules are laid out as the story progresses, telling us the tales of love and compassion. It is also a tale of self-discovery where the characters understand themselves better by coming in contact with Shams. 

The present follows a housewife, Ella, in Massachusetts. In her first editing job, she reads a book by an author named Aziz Z. Zahara. She starts seeing Tubriz in him and discovers passion and spiritual awakening. 

<em>"Do not chase love outside—discover and remove the walls within your own heart."</em>

I found both stories compelling. Both eras are unsettling, with religious disbelieve, social turmoil and the hearts of both Rumi and Ella are lovelorn, where Shams becomes the guide for Rumi and Aziz becomes the one for Ella. 

Shafak is a great storyteller. Through this novel, she offers her insights, which are timeless and relatable across cultures.
